Agentic AI · Scientific computing

May 2026–Present

Verifiable agents for scientific workflows

Dr. Haohan Wang · University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

I am developing a compiler-inspired biomedical agent architecture that translates scientific requests into explicit, typed plans. The system connects each step to a registered computational capability and checks execution and scientific validity before authorizing a result.

  • Planning and input interpretation. Resolving the roles of matrices, cohorts, conditions, covariates, and annotations, with typed execution contracts for H5AD, MTX, VCF, and BED inputs.
  • Reusable scientific capabilities. Building support for differential expression, gene regulatory and co-expression networks, enrichment, and variant or peak annotation across single-cell, transcriptomic, and genomic workflows.
  • Verification and recovery. Developing independent recomputation, provenance checks, adversarial tests, and repeat-run comparisons, with authorization withheld when required checks fail.

Related presentation: A General Biomedical Agentic Framework · MMLS 2026.

Ongoing research; manuscript in preparation for submission to RECOMB 2027.

Multimodal learning · Healthcare AI

Sep 2025–Aug 2026

Multimodal EHR modeling & clinical NLP

Dr. Fenglong Ma · Penn State University

This project examines how clinical time series and text can be combined for prediction when some information is missing. My work focused on a modular learning pipeline, transformer-based fusion, and analysis of model reliability across different input conditions.

  • Data and model pipelines. Standardized schemas and dataloaders for EHR time series and clinical text, with explicit support for missing modalities through dropout and imputation.
  • Multimodal fusion. Modality-specific encoders and a masked-attention fusion transformer, with a shared configuration and training interface for comparisons against single-modality, early-fusion, and late-fusion baselines.
  • Reliability and interpretation. Evaluation work covering calibration, modality and feature attribution, ablations, and robustness to missing inputs and distribution shift.

Supported in Summer 2026 by the SURE fellowship under Dr. Ma.

Explainable AI · Computer vision

Nov 2024–Aug 2025

Explainable gait abnormality detection

Dr. Suman Saha · Penn State University

I worked on CNN-LSTM models for gait analysis using the GAVD and OU-MVLP datasets. The study uses joint-based features and silhouette sequences, with SHAP and Grad-CAM to examine the temporal and spatial information informing predictions.

  • Developed PyTorch training pipelines and compared classical machine-learning and deep-learning baselines.
  • Prepared sequence data, addressed class imbalance with SMOTE and loss weighting, and evaluated on unseen identities.

Planned thesis research

Reliable adaptation under distribution shift

I am interested in estimating, without target labels, whether adapting a pretrained model will improve or degrade its performance. The planned direction explores reliability signals for deciding when to adapt and when to retain the original model.
