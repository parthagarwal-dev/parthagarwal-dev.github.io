Multimodal AI · Missing-modality robustness

Multimodal EHR modeling and clinical prediction

Funded SURE fellowship · Summer 2026

Undergraduate research · Dr. Fenglong Ma
Penn State University · Sep 2025–Aug 2026

How can clinical prediction models combine complementary information while remaining useful when some inputs are missing?

Research problem

My work with Dr. Fenglong Ma studied how multimodal clinical models behave when information is incomplete. A concrete case study used the EHRSHOT hyponatremia prediction task, examining how different EHR sources contribute to prediction and how their absence changes model behavior.

Task
Hyponatremia prediction using EHRSHOT
Inputs
Laboratory measurements, medications, clinical codes, visits, and note metadata
Evaluation
Discrimination, probability error, calibration, and controlled removal of input sources

The evaluated August 2026 setup used note metadata; narrative clinical text was unavailable. Clinical NLP and text encoders belong to the broader research direction and are not inputs to the results reported below.

My contribution & design rationale

Build a consistent prediction-time data pipeline

I worked on standardized data preparation and modality-specific features, using a frozen cache of 206,005 prediction events with five input sources. The setup used records from a 30-day window before each prediction time, making the information available to the model explicit.

Represent missing inputs during learning

I worked on modality-specific representations, masked-attention fusion, and explicit missing-modality handling. The V1 comparison trained the same model family with standard training and with modality dropout, then removed individual sources at evaluation time to examine their contribution.

Measure different aspects of reliability

I examined AUROC/AUPRC, Brier score, calibration, and modality-removal ablations. This separates changes in ranking performance from changes in predicted probabilities and dependence on particular sources. My broader analysis work also included feature attribution, error analysis, and comparison with pretrained EHR representations.

Preliminary findings · August 2026

Modality dropout and missing laboratory inputs

In a recorded V1 experiment, training with modality dropout produced similar full-input AUROC and a lower Brier score. When laboratory inputs were removed, the AUROC drop was smaller: 0.0206 with modality dropout, compared with 0.0381 under standard training.

Research takeaway: Full-input discrimination alone gives an incomplete view of multimodal learning. In this experiment, the clearest change was in probability error and sensitivity to missing laboratory data, motivating evaluation that reports these outcomes separately.
