Multimodal AI · Clinical NLP

Multimodal EHR modeling and clinical prediction

Funded SURE fellowship · Summer 2026

Undergraduate research · Dr. Fenglong Ma
Penn State University · Sep 2025–Aug 2026

How can clinical prediction models combine complementary information while remaining useful when some inputs are missing?

Research problem

Clinical time series and free-text records offer different views of a patient’s history. Their availability can vary, so a multimodal model needs an explicit way to represent missing information and assess how much each input contributes. This project examined that problem through a common data, modeling, and evaluation pipeline.

My contributions

Data preparation and reproducible training

My work included standardized schemas and dataloaders for EHR time series and clinical text, along with a shared configuration and training interface. Missing-modality handling included dropout and imputation so that different input conditions could be studied consistently.

Masked-attention fusion

I worked on a transformer architecture with separate modality encoders and masked-attention fusion. The comparison setup included single-modality models and early- and late-fusion baselines to examine the contribution of multimodal integration.

Reliability and interpretability

Evaluation work covered predictive discrimination and calibration, ablations, and robustness under missingness and distribution shift. Interpretation methods included attention rollout and SHAP for modality, token, and feature attribution.

SURE research fellowship

I completed a funded, ten-week, full-time Summer Undergraduate Research Experience (SURE) fellowship under Dr. Ma from May to July 2026 through Penn State’s Eberly College of Science. The fellowship supported literature synthesis, hypothesis formulation, controlled experimentation, and ablation and error analysis.
