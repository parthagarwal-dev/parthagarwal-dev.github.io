Agentic AI · AI for science

Verifiable agentic AI for computational biology

Manuscript under submission · RECOMB 2027

Undergraduate research · Dr. Haohan Wang
University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · May 2026–Present

How can scientific agents produce results whose computational and scientific validity can be checked independently?

Research problem

Scientific analysis requires more than choosing and running a tool. An agent must interpret biological roles, select suitable methods, preserve the meaning of the data, and determine whether the output supports the requested conclusion. My work studies an architecture that makes these decisions explicit and checkable.

My contributions

Typed planning and semantic input resolution

I am developing the translation from scientific intent to typed execution plans. This includes resolving matrices, cohorts, conditions, covariates, annotations, and method parameters across H5AD, MTX, VCF, and BED inputs. Execution contracts make required inputs and expected outputs explicit before a computational step runs.

A reusable scientific capability layer

I am building registered capabilities for pseudobulk differential expression, regulatory-network inference with GENIE3 and GRNBoost2, co-expression analysis with WGCNA, enrichment, and variant or peak annotation. Current extensions include epigenomic input resolution and motif-related analysis. These components support reuse across workflows rather than requiring a separate implementation for every request.

Independent verification and recovery

Verification work includes recomputing key quantities, checking provenance and hashes, testing adversarial inputs, and comparing repeated executions. The architecture withholds authorization when required checks fail and supports recovery before a result is released.

Research status

Manuscript under submission to RECOMB 2027. The research is ongoing.

MMLS 2026 poster

I presented General-Purpose Agentic Framework for Biomedical Research with Dr. Haohan Wang at the Midwest Machine Learning Symposium, held at Purdue University on June 24–25, 2026. The poster introduces a planner–router–verifier approach, reusable biomedical tool primitives, and a gene regulatory network case study.
